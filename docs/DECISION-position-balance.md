# Decision — Position Balance: DELTA Keeps Its Own Exchange Rate (10 Nov 2026)

**Steve, 10 Nov:** "yeah let's lock in A … I'll keep thinking on the TE stuff."

## The Choice

- **A (chosen):** keep DELTA's own exchange rate between positions. This is the rate at which the Own Value Scale
  trades a QB against an RB, WR or TE.
- **B (not chosen):** borrow only the market's balance between positions, and keep DELTA's order within each position.

## Why A

- **DELTA's TE values match what TEs went on to deliver:** 99% on average, 2013–2022.
- **The case for B rested on elite TEs being underpriced,** and history can neither confirm nor rule that out. Steve's
  point is that the TE1 is the one unique edge at a position where TE5 is barely above an average starter. Two tests
  looked at it:
  - `PREREG-ovs-elite.md`: the crash test failed. With only 30 elite TE seasons, even a 25% shortchange is invisible.
  - `PREREG-ovs-peers.md` (8 Nov): matching elite players only to their closest peers predicted worse at every
    position.
- **Borrowing the market's balance** because the rankings look wrong would put market consensus into DELTA's value.

## What Was Learned Along The Way

- **Elite TEs stay elite more often than any other position.** A top-3 finisher (2013–2022) was top 3 again the next
  season:
  - TE 40%
  - WR 27%
  - RB 23%
  - QB 17%
- **Bowers' 30 matches** (week 9, full PPR; he ranks 38th, TE2):
  - **On average they produced 12% less than him.**
  - **Half the weight sits on 2016+ TEs,** such as Andrews, Kittle, Hooper, Henry and Reed. His Gronkowski, Gates and
    Gonzalez matches are older seasons, so they carry little weight.
  - **13% of the weight sits on matches who delivered zero,** including Aaron Hernandez 2013, whose career ended for
    non-football reasons.
- **What-if, not a test:** counting only his 13 elite-production matches would put him about 26th.

## Open
